Methodology

How the setup score is calculated

Setup score is a composite 0–100+ number. Every triggered component adds points; higher = stronger setup.

ComponentPoints
20-day high (breakout above prior 20-session high)+10
55-day high+20 (added on top of 20-day)
52-week high (252-day)+30 (added on top of 20 and 55)
Horizontal resistance break (tested cluster of swing highs)+25
Volatility contraction break (ATR compression → range expansion)+25
Volume confirmation bonus(vol_multiple − 1.5) × 10, capped at +15
Base length bonus (days consolidating before breakout)days ÷ 5, capped at +10
Fundamental quality (Piotroski F-score)score × 2, capped at +18 (not applied in manual mode)
Trend context bonus (200 SMA rising AND within 15% of 52W high)+10
Multi-day streak bonus (consecutive days on report)(streak − 1) × 5, capped at +15

Reading scores. Only setups scoring ≥40 are shown. As a rough guide:

  • 40–59: Emerging setup. One or two patterns triggered without much confirmation. Worth watching; not a primary trade.
  • 60–79: Solid setup. Multiple patterns and/or strong volume, decent base length or streak.
  • 80+: High conviction. Multiple breakout patterns triggering together with volume, base length, and streak confirmation. Rare but strongest historical hit rates.

Why multi-day streak matters. The daily-run / weekend-review cadence exists so that stocks appearing 2–3 days in a row get flagged as stronger. First-day breakouts fail often; genuine ones tend to hold and re-appear.

How market breadth is calculated

Market breadth is the average of 5 sub-indicators, each normalised to 0–10:

  • % of eligible pool above 200-day SMA — linear: 0% → 0, 100% → 10
  • % of eligible pool above 50-day SMA — linear
  • New 52-week highs vs new 52-week lows ratio — 5:1 → 10, 1:1 → 5, 1:5 → 0
  • SPY 14-day rate of change — −5% → 0, 0% → 5, +5% → 10
  • VIX (inverted) — VIX 10 → 10, VIX 40 → 0

Weekly = today's snapshot. Monthly = 20-day rolling average of daily weekly-scores.

Verdict thresholds:

  • Adverse (<4): Broad market weakness. Even good setups fail more often in this regime. Consider standing aside or sizing smaller.
  • Neutral (4–7): Normal environment. Trade setups on their own merits with standard risk management.
  • Favorable (>7): Broad participation. Higher hit rate on breakouts. Look for setups actively.
Column reference
  • Score. Composite setup quality (see above). Sorted descending.
  • Patterns. Which breakout patterns triggered today. Multiple = stronger.
  • Entry. Today's closing price — your reference entry.
  • Stop. Suggested initial stop-loss. Computed as the tighter of (breakout price × 0.93) or the 20-day swing low. This caps risk near ~7% while still respecting a tight base if one exists.
  • TP1 / TP2. Take-profit targets at 2R and 3R, where R = entry − stop. TP1 is your first partial-exit target; TP2 is the runner target.
  • R:R. Reward-to-risk multiple at TP1. Higher = more attractive risk profile.
  • Piotroski. 0–9 fundamental quality score (green ≥ 7, red ≤ 3). Shown as "—" in manual mode — quality was pre-filtered by your screener.
  • Streak. Number of consecutive days this stock has appeared on the report. "×3" means today is the third consecutive appearance. Higher = more confirmation.
  • Sector. From your screener CSV, or "Unknown" if the column wasn't included.
Confluence layer (gates applied before any signal reaches the report)

Every signal must survive three independent gates in addition to the pattern checks. The idea is to filter out breakouts that lack supporting context — momentum, relative strength, or sector tailwinds.

Gate 1 — Momentum confluence. At least 3 of 4 must hold:

  • RSI(14) in 50–75 — strong but not extended
  • ADX(14) ≥ 20 — stock is actually trending, not chopping
  • Weekly MACD histogram positive — higher-timeframe alignment
  • Price in top 40% of 52-week range — already in a strength regime

Gate 2 — Relative Strength percentile. Each stock's 12-week return minus SPY's 12-week return is percentile-ranked across the eligible pool. Only tickers in the top 25% (RS ≥ 75) pass. Tickers in the top 10% (RS ≥ 90) get a +10 scoring bonus. This is the single most predictive filter for breakout follow-through.

Gate 3 — Sector regime. The 11 Sector SPDR ETFs (XLK, XLV, XLF, …) are ranked by 60-day return vs SPY. Only tickers whose sector ranks in the top 6 of 11 pass. Top-3 sectors award a +5 scoring bonus. Unmapped sectors (e.g., "Unknown") pass by default.

Market regime adjustment. When weekly breadth is Adverse (< 4), the minimum shown score is raised from 40 to 75 — only the strongest setups surface during broad weakness.

Hidden bonus. If all 4 momentum checks pass (not just 3), the setup earns an additional +5 scoring bonus.

How Recent Quality Breakouts is ranked

The Recent Quality Breakouts section aggregates every signal from the past 7 days (rolling week, same rollover cadence as the Positional Trading Tool), then picks the top 10 by a composite keep score:

ComponentContribution
Ticker's best score ever achieved (base)full value
Recency bonus (latest appearance)+15 within 7 days, +5 within 21 days, 0 older
Appearance bonus (times seen in archive)+2 per appearance, capped at +15
Volume bonus (max multiple observed)+5 if ≥ 2× avg, +10 if ≥ 3× avg

Eligibility: ticker's peak score must be ≥ 60 (the "solid setup" tier). Fresh tickers hitting 60+ enter the ranking; low-quality signals never do.

Effect of the composite:

  • High-conviction stays sticky. A 90-score signal from three weeks ago still outranks a 65-score fresh signal.
  • Repeat winners rise. A ticker seen 6 times climbs above one-offs, all else equal.
  • Volume-confirmed setups get priority over low-volume ones at the same score.
  • Recency still matters — but as a tiebreaker, not a dominant factor.

Row data: the Pick Date is when the ticker's best score fired; Seen is total appearances in the archive; Entry / Stop / TPs are from that peak signal. Always sanity-check the current price against these — they can be days or weeks old.

How Supertrend Longs is built and scored

The Supertrend-long section surfaces tickers where a fresh long flip has fired on the daily chart within the last SUPERTREND_MAX_FLIP_AGE_DAYS trading days (default 20). Supertrend is a trailing-stop indicator: when price is above the line, the trend is long and the line itself is your exit level. Uses the same parameters as the Positional Trading Tool: ATR period 10, multiplier 2.5 (matches TradingView free tier).

Why the flip-age gate matters: without it, every sustained long trend keeps appearing day after day. The point of this section is "new signals I didn't see yesterday," not "every stock currently in a trend." Stale but still-strong trends live in the Recent Quality Supertrend history section instead.

A ticker enters the pool when (a) it passed trend + behavioral + momentum confluence gates in Pass 1, (b) Supertrend direction is currently long, and (c) flip age ≤ 20 days. Same RS + sector gates apply as for breakouts and watchlist.

Supertrend score (0 to ~122):

ComponentPoints

RS strength (percentile vs eligible pool)rs_percentile ÷ 2, up to +50

How to read the row:

  • Flip Age — bars since the long signal fired. 0d = flipped today, 1-3d = fresh entry zone, > 15d = mature trend (good for riding, worse for entry)
  • Current — today's close
  • ST Stop — the Supertrend trailing-stop line. Exit if price closes below this.
  • % Above Stop — how far current price sits above the stop. Smaller = tighter risk, bigger = more room to breathe but also more at risk if stop is hit

Why this complements breakouts and watchlist:

  • Breakouts = specific chart pattern just fired (point-in-time trigger)
  • Watchlist = quality setup building, waiting for trigger (pre-trigger)
  • Supertrend = already in a trend, trailing-stop managed (continuation)

A ticker appearing in multiple sections (e.g., both Breakouts and Supertrend) is confluence — multiple signal types confirming the same name. Those are often the best setups.

Recent Quality Supertrend (history) aggregates the past 7 days (rolling week) of Supertrend-long entries. Only tickers whose peak Supertrend score ever hit 70+ in that window are eligible. Keep score adds recency, repeat appearances, and accumulation bonuses.

How Support & Resistance, Break & Retest, and Reversal sections work

Shared quality base. These three sections scan every ticker, not just main trend-filter survivors, because their setups are often mid-pullback. Each ticker must still pass: no −10% gap-down in 20 sessions, and close above a 200 SMA that is higher than 20 sessions ago. Each section then applies its own RS and sector gates, a minimum score, and a display cap, so only the technically strongest names show.

Support & Resistance levels. Swing highs and lows (5 bars each side) from the last 120 sessions are clustered: pivots within 1.5% merge into one horizontal level. A level needs ≥ 2 touches. Old highs and old lows are pooled, so a former ceiling can show up as today's floor (role reversal). "Near" = within 3%.

Break & Retest. Finds the most recent close above the prior 55-session high on ≥ 1.5× volume, 1–20 sessions ago (same-day breakouts are in Today's Breakouts). It is a retest when price has pulled back ≥ 3% from its post-breakout high, a low in the last 3 sessions came within 2% of the breakout level, and today's close is still at or above the level. Otherwise it is holding above. A close more than 3% below the level marks a failed breakout, which is dropped. Score: retest 25 (holding 10), bounce today +10, breakout volume above 1.5× up to +15, freshness up to +10, extended > 10% −10, plus RS ÷ 2, momentum × 5, sector. Gates: RS ≥ 70, sector ≤ 6.

Reversal Swing. A pullback inside an intact uptrend: down 5–25% from the 20-day high, RSI(14) at or below 40 within the last 5 sessions, and today's candle is a bullish engulfing, an outside reversal (undercut prior low, closed above prior high), or a hammer (lower wick ≥ 2× body, close in top third). Score: trigger 15–20, pullback depth in the 8–15% sweet spot +15, RSI rebound ≥ 5 points +10, pullback low within 2% of the 50 SMA +10, today's volume ≥ 1.2× average +10, plus RS ÷ 2 and sector. Gates: RS ≥ 55, sector ≤ 7. Entry = today's close, stop = 1% under the 5-day low, TP1 = 2R; the prior 20-day high is the natural target.

How these differ from existing sections: Watchlist triggers are simple 20/55-day highs; Near Resistance uses multi-touch horizontal levels. Recovery is a 20/200 EMA turnaround from a downtrend; Reversal Swing is a short pullback inside an existing uptrend. These sections are recomputed from price history each day and are not archived.

How Recovery Setups are built and scored

Recovery surfaces stocks "coming out of correction" — the 20 EMA has recently crossed above the 200 EMA from below and the 20 EMA is still close to the 200 EMA (not yet extended). These are classic turnaround setups: a stock went through a correction, found a bottom, and is now rebuilding its uptrend.

Entry conditions (all must hold):

  • 20 EMA currently above 200 EMA
  • 20 EMA within 10% above 200 EMA (not yet extended)
  • Crossover from below happened within the last 30 trading days
  • 20 EMA is rising (compared to 5 bars ago)
  • No -10% gap-down in last 20 sessions (behavioral filter)
  • Momentum confluence ≥ 3 of 4

Looser gates than other sections: Recovery accepts RS ≥ 60 (not 85) and sector top 7 (not 4). Why: a stock that just emerged from a correction has not yet rebuilt its relative strength — if we required RS ≥ 85, we'd never catch the turnaround at the inflection point, only after it had already run.

Recovery score (0 to ~142):

ComponentPoints
Freshness (days since cross)+30 if ≤ 5d, +25 if ≤ 10d, +15 if ≤ 20d, +5 if ≤ 30d
Closeness (gap between EMAs)+20 if ≤ 3%, +15 if ≤ 5%, +10 if ≤ 7%, +5 if ≤ 10%
RS strengthrs_percentile ÷ 2, up to +50
Momentum confluence passes+5 per check, up to +20
Sector strength bonus+2 per rank above 7, up to +12
Accumulation days+1 per day, up to +10

How to read the row:

  • Cross Age — days since 20 EMA crossed 200 EMA. Smaller = fresher = better. Highlighted green if ≤ 5 days.
  • EMA Gap — how far 20 EMA sits above 200 EMA. Smaller = closer to inflection point = better. Highlighted green if ≤ 3%.
  • Trade entry mechanics: look for the first pullback into the 20 EMA, or wait for a close above the recent swing high. Stop below the 200 EMA (or below recent swing low). Target: measured move from the correction depth, or trail with the 20 EMA.

Why this fills a gap: Breakouts and Supertrend both require strength (recent highs, trending long). Recovery catches names BEFORE they get strong — the turnaround phase. These setups can have outsized reward if the recovery continues, but have higher failure rates than confirmed breakouts, so size smaller.

How the Swing-Ready Watchlist is built and scored

The watchlist surfaces stocks that passed every quality gate (trend, behavioral, momentum confluence, RS percentile, sector regime) but didn't trigger any breakout pattern today. These are "stalking list" setups — quality candidates waiting for the trigger. Entering on the trigger (via buy-stop or alert) often gives better R:R than chasing a breakout already in motion.

Watchlist score (0 to ~112):

Pending Trigger / Stop / TPs: the Trigger is the nearest resistance (20-day high; 55-day as fallback). Stop and TPs are computed from the trigger using the same logic as a fired breakout (stop = tighter of 7% below trigger or 20-day swing low; TP1 = 2R, TP2 = 3R). These are inactive until price actually breaks the Trigger — set alerts and let the setup come to you.

Recent Quality Watchlist (history) aggregates the past 7 days (rolling week) of watchlist entries. Only tickers whose peak watchlist score ever hit 70+ in that window are eligible. Composite ranking (keep score) adds bonuses for recency, repeat appearances, and accumulation.

What to look for:

  • % to Go close to 0 (e.g., < 3%): the trigger is imminent — prime alert candidate
  • AccDays ≥ 7: clear institutional accumulation under the surface
