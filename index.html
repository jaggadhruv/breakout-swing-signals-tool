Methodology

How the setup score is calculated

Setup score is a composite 0–100+ number. Every triggered component adds points; higher = stronger setup.

ComponentPoints
20-day high (breakout above prior 20-session high)+10
55-day high+20 (added on top of 20-day)
52-week high (252-day)+30 (added on top of 20 and 55)
Horizontal resistance break (tested cluster of swing highs)+25
Volatility contraction break (ATR compression → range expansion)+25
Volume confirmation bonus(vol_multiple − 1.5) × 10, capped at +15
Base length bonus (days consolidating before breakout)days ÷ 5, capped at +10
Fundamental quality (Piotroski F-score)score × 2, capped at +18 (not applied in manual mode)
Trend context bonus (200 SMA rising AND within 15% of 52W high)+10
Multi-day streak bonus (consecutive days on report)(streak − 1) × 5, capped at +15

Reading scores. Only setups scoring ≥40 are shown. As a rough guide:

  • 40–59: Emerging setup. One or two patterns triggered without much confirmation. Worth watching; not a primary trade.
  • 60–79: Solid setup. Multiple patterns and/or strong volume, decent base length or streak.
  • 80+: High conviction. Multiple breakout patterns triggering together with volume, base length, and streak confirmation. Rare but strongest historical hit rates.

Why multi-day streak matters. The daily-run / weekend-review cadence exists so that stocks appearing 2–3 days in a row get flagged as stronger. First-day breakouts fail often; genuine ones tend to hold and re-appear.

How market breadth is calculated

Market breadth is the average of 5 sub-indicators, each normalised to 0–10:

  • % of eligible pool above 200-day SMA — linear: 0% → 0, 100% → 10
  • % of eligible pool above 50-day SMA — linear
  • New 52-week highs vs new 52-week lows ratio — 5:1 → 10, 1:1 → 5, 1:5 → 0

Weekly = today's snapshot. Monthly = 20-day rolling average of daily weekly-scores.

Verdict thresholds:

  • Adverse (<4): Broad market weakness. Even good setups fail more often in this regime. Consider standing aside or sizing smaller.
  • Neutral (4–7): Normal environment. Trade setups on their own merits with standard risk management.
  • Favorable (>7): Broad participation. Higher hit rate on breakouts. Look for setups actively.
Column reference

Confluence layer (gates applied before any signal reaches the report)

Every signal must survive three independent gates in addition to the pattern checks. The idea is to filter out breakouts that lack supporting context — momentum, relative strength, or sector tailwinds.

Gate 1 — Momentum confluence. At least 3 of 4 must hold:

  • RSI(14) in 50–75 — strong but not extended
  • ADX(14) ≥ 20 — stock is actually trending, not chopping
  • Weekly MACD histogram positive — higher-timeframe alignment
  • Price in top 40% of 52-week range — already in a strength regime

Gate 2 — Relative Strength percentile. Each stock's 12-week return minus SPY's 12-week return is percentile-ranked across the eligible pool. Only tickers in the top 25% (RS ≥ 75) pass. Tickers in the top 10% (RS ≥ 90) get a +10 scoring bonus. This is the single most predictive filter for breakout follow-through.

Gate 3 — Sector regime. The 11 Sector SPDR ETFs (XLK, XLV, XLF, …) are ranked by 60-day return vs SPY. Only tickers whose sector ranks in the top 6 of 11 pass. Top-3 sectors award a +5 scoring bonus. Unmapped sectors (e.g., "Unknown") pass by default.

Market regime adjustment. When weekly breadth is Adverse (< 4), the minimum shown score is raised from 40 to 75 — only the strongest setups surface during broad weakness.

Hidden bonus. If all 4 momentum checks pass (not just 3), the setup earns an additional +5 scoring bonus.

Data-health checks (why a run may refuse to publish)

Bad-bar repair. If Yahoo returns a final daily bar with missing or impossible prices (blank, zero, or a close outside the day's high-low range), up to 3 such bars are removed and the previous complete session is used. The count appears as "repaired" in the data line.

Fail: the run stops and publishes nothing (the previous good report stays live, and no history, archive or log changes) if any of these hold:

  • fewer than 80% of tickers downloaded;
  • more than 40% of tickers have a latest bar older than SPY's;
  • breadth is internally contradictory (with ≥ 50 tickers): 0% above both the 50- and 200-day averages; or new highs dominating while under 10% are above the 50-day average; or new lows dominating while over 90% are above it.

Warn: the report publishes with an amber banner if 80–95% of tickers downloaded, more than 10% are a session behind, or more than 10% needed a broken bar repaired.

Every report shows a data line under the header: tickers downloaded, latest market bar date, stale and repaired counts, and which symbols returned no data (often delisted or renamed tickers to fix in your CSV).

How Recovery Setups are built and scored

Recovery surfaces stocks "coming out of correction" — the 20 EMA has recently crossed above the 200 EMA from below and the 20 EMA is still close to the 200 EMA (not yet extended). These are classic turnaround setups: a stock went through a correction, found a bottom, and is now rebuilding its uptrend.

Entry conditions (all must hold):

  • 20 EMA currently above 200 EMA
  • 20 EMA within 10% above 200 EMA (not yet extended)
  • Crossover from below happened within the last 30 trading days
  • 20 EMA is rising (compared to 5 bars ago)
  • No -10% gap-down in last 20 sessions (behavioral filter)
  • Momentum confluence ≥ 3 of 4

Looser gates than other sections: Recovery accepts RS ≥ 60 (not 85) and sector top 7 (not 4). Why: a stock that just emerged from a correction has not yet rebuilt its relative strength — if we required RS ≥ 85, we'd never catch the turnaround at the inflection point, only after it had already run.
